[1,3,13,14]<#refs>Equivalence classes of self-dual codes over the Kleinian four group. [12]<#refs>Orbits of simple undirected graphs under local complementation. [1,4,7,8,10,11]<#refs>Equivalence classes of isotropic systems. [4]<#refs>Equivalence classes of zero-dimensional quantum codes. [5–11]<#refs>LU-equivalence classes of quantum graph states. [9,10,11]<#refs>Equivalence classes of quadratic Boolean functions under certain transformations. [15]<#refs>For a more detailed description of the results, see the paper [1]<#refs>, or my Master's thesis [2]<#refs>.Zero-dimensional [[n,0,d]] quantum stabilizer codes corresponds to additive (n,2n,d) codes over GF(4) whichare self-dual with respect to the Hermitian trace inner product [3]<#refs>. Zero-dimensional quantum codesrepresent highly entangled single quantum states.Every self-dual additive code over GF(4) can be represented by a simple undirected graph[4–11]<#refs>. Thegraph with adjacency matrix Γ represents the code with generator matrix Γ + ωI. Local complementation, asimple  graph  operation,  generates  the  orbits  of  equivalent  self-dual  additive  codes  over  GF(4)[4,7,8,10,11]<#refs>.Using LC, we can generate the orbits of all inequivalent self-dual additive codes over GF(4) of length n. Thesecodes have previously been classified by Calderbank et al. [3]<#refs> (up to n=5), by Hein et al. [9]<#refs> (upto n=7), by H�hn [12]<#refs> (up to n=7) and by Glynn et al. [8]<#refs> (up to n=9). For higher lengths, onlyoptimal codes have been classified [13,14]<#refs>. We have generated all LC orbits for n up to 12, and thisdatabase contains a representative of each orbit. The members of an LC orbit are counted up to isomorphism.(We use the program nauty<https://web.archive.org/web/20240803110852/http://cs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/nauty/> to checkfor graph isomorphism.)A  decomposable code  can  be  expressed  as  the  direct  sum  of  two  smaller  codes.  Indecomposable  codescorrespond  to  connected  graphs.  The  decomposable  codes  can  easily  be  constructed  by  combiningindecomposable codes of shorter length. A self-dual additive code over GF(4) is of Type II if all codewordshave even weight.
